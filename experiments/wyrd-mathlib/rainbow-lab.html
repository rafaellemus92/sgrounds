Rainbow Ray Lab

A Lean formalization of classical rainbow geometry

Sunlight enters a raindrop, bends, reflects once or twice inside, and leaves. Lean proves where that turning is smallest. That ray is the bow, and from it come the colour order and a gap between the two bows. Drag the ray and slide the colour to see each result. Every statement on this page carries one of three labels:

The sky

Two bows, two colour orders, and a gap

Each arc is the bow ray for one index between red (1.331) and violet (1.343), drawn at its computed angle. Lean proves the order of the arcs and the 1° brackets at the two ends. Between 43° and 50° the once- and twice-reflected ray families leave a gap. That is a gap in those two families, not an absence of all light.

The model behind the proofs.

Lean takes the turning angle D(i) = kπ + 2i − 2(k+1)·arcsin(sin i / n) as a definition. It assumes a perfect spherical drop, ray optics, a point sun, one real index per colour, and entry angles from 0° to 90°. The indices 1.331 (red), 4/3 (water) and 1.343 (violet) are inputs. The theorems bound the signed angles π − D and D − π. Those equal the angle you see for every ray only when 1.18 ≤ n ≤ √2, which this page computes but Lean does not prove.

Note on prior work.
The mathematics is classical: Descartes (1637) and Newton (Opticks, 1704). As of 22 October 2026, we found no earlier machine-checked proof of these rainbow results.
  • We searched Lean and Mathlib, Physlib, Isabelle, HOL Light, HOL4, Rocq, Mizar, Metamath, ACL2, PVS and Agda.
  • The closest formal work is the HOL Light optics library of Siddique, Aravantinos and Tahar, which treats refraction only in the small-angle approximation. In Lean, FormalPhysics derives Snell's law at a flat surface.
  • GitHub code search was unavailable to us, and some papers could only be read as abstracts.
